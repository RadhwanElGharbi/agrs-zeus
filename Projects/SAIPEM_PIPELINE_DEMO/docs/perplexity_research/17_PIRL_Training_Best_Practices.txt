The best practices for training **Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning (RL) models for spatial optimization** involve a combination of curriculum learning, transfer learning, careful hyperparameter tuning, well-defined convergence criteria, and adequate computational resources. Validation of physical constraints is critical to ensure model reliability.

### Curriculum Learning Strategies
- **Progressive Complexity:** Start training on simpler or short-term sub-problems and gradually increase complexity or time horizon. This approach, inspired by human learning, helps the model stabilize and avoid local minima during training[1][2][3].
- **Dynamic Curriculum Regularization:** Adjust the difficulty dynamically based on the model’s current performance, for example by monitoring the PDE residuals or the L2 distance between predicted and target solutions. This prevents propagation of high-error regions and adapts training to solution complexity[2][3].
- **Self-Paced Learning:** Use training loss as a difficulty measure to select samples from easy to hard automatically, accelerating convergence and improving training efficiency[5].

### Transfer Learning from Simpler Environments
- Train the RL agent first on a simplified or surrogate physical model (e.g., a reduced-order or idealized system) to learn basic dynamics and control policies.
- Transfer the learned policy or network weights to the more complex target environment, fine-tuning with fewer samples or simulations. This reduces computational cost and improves sample efficiency[1][4].
- For example, a policy trained on a nonholonomic system (Chaplygin sleigh) was transferred to a fluid simulation for a swimming robot, significantly reducing simulation time and improving control precision[4].

### Hyperparameter Tuning
- Tune learning rates, batch sizes, and network architectures carefully to balance convergence speed and stability.
- Use early stopping based on maximum PDE residuals or policy performance metrics to avoid overfitting or divergence[2].
- Adjust PDE parameter step sizes dynamically in curriculum learning to handle varying solution complexities[2][3].
- Regularization parameters in physics-informed loss terms should be balanced to enforce physical constraints without overwhelming data fitting.

### Convergence Criteria
- Monitor **PDE residuals** or physics-based loss terms to ensure the solution respects governing equations.
- Use **L2 norm errors** between predicted and reference solutions (analytical or high-fidelity numerical) as convergence indicators[2][3].
- For RL, track policy reward stability and constraint satisfaction over episodes.
- Employ early stopping when residuals or errors plateau or start increasing, indicating convergence or overfitting[2].

### Computational Requirements
- Physics-informed RL models are computationally intensive due to the need for solving PDEs or simulating physics at each training step.
- Curriculum and transfer learning reduce computational load by focusing training on simpler tasks first and reusing learned features[1][4].
- Parallelization and GPU acceleration are often necessary for practical training times.
- Lightweight collocation point distribution methods can reduce computational overhead in PINNs training[6].

### Validation of Physical Constraints
- Incorporate physical laws directly into the loss function (e.g., PDE residuals, boundary conditions) to enforce constraints during training.
- Validate by checking residuals of governing equations on unseen test data or simulation scenarios.
- Use **constraint violation metrics** to quantify how well the model respects physical laws.
- Perform **benchmarking against analytical solutions or high-fidelity simulations** to ensure physical realism.
- In RL, verify that the learned policy respects nonholonomic or conservation constraints by simulating trajectories and checking constraint adherence[4].
