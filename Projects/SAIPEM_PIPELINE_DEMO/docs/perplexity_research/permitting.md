### Pipeline Permits and Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

- The permitting process for pipelines in Italy, including those crossing Lazio and Abruzzo, follows the framework established by **EU Regulation No 347/2013 (TEN-E Regulation)** and national laws implementing the EU EIA Directive (Directive 2011/92/EU). This involves a **pre-application phase** with scoping reports and route identification, followed by submission and acceptance of the full application by competent authorities[1][2].

- For example, the **Larino-Chieti pipeline** in Abruzzo required a full EIA due to its environmental sensitivity. The EIA assessed impacts during construction (noise, dust, traffic, waste disposal, and access restrictions) and operation, considering the crossing of areas with hydraulic and landslide hazards, archaeological sites, and multiple **Natura 2000 protected areas**. Mitigation and avoidance measures were integrated into the project design to minimize environmental damage[2].

- The **Adriatica Pipeline**, which passes through Abruzzo and Lazio among other regions, has faced environmental scrutiny due to its route through the Central Apennines, a seismically active and environmentally sensitive area. Local communities and environmental groups have protested the pipeline, citing risks from earthquakes and environmental degradation[3][5].

- Environmental assessments and authorizations in Italy are regionally managed, with regional authorities (Regione Lazio, Regione Abruzzo) responsible for issuing development consents based on EIA outcomes. These consents include conditions to avoid, prevent, or reduce significant adverse environmental effects and may be challenged in regional administrative courts on formal or substantial grounds[6].

### Right of Way and Land Acquisition

- The acquisition of **right of way** and land for pipeline construction in Lazio and Abruzzo involves negotiations with landowners and may require expropriation under Italian law if voluntary agreements cannot be reached. The process is closely linked to the permitting and environmental authorization phases, ensuring that land use complies with environmental and planning regulations[2][6].

- Pipeline projects must consider protected areas, archaeological sites, and hazard zones, which can complicate right of way acquisition and require additional mitigation or route adjustments[2][3].
