### Industry Standards and Guidelines

- **AACE International (Cost Estimate Classification 18R-97)** recommends contingency based on estimate class and project maturity, with Class 1 estimates having contingencies as low as 5-10%, and early Class 4-5 estimates requiring 15-30% contingency to cover uncertainties[1][3][10].

- **PMI (Project Management Institute)** emphasizes risk-based contingency reserves derived from quantitative risk analysis (e.g., Monte Carlo simulation) to achieve confidence levels such as P60 or P90, with contingency increasing significantly for higher confidence (e.g., P90 contingency can be 6% or more of base cost, versus less than 1% at P60)[1][6].

- **Major pipeline operators** and infrastructure projects typically apply a **total contingency of 10-20%** of base construction cost, broken down by risk categories. For example, a 6% contingency at P90 confidence level is common for well-defined projects, while early-stage estimates may require up to 18% contingency[1][5].

### Additional Context

- Contingency is distinct from allowances or management reserves and is specifically intended to address **known unknowns** (risks identified but not fully quantified) rather than scope changes or omissions[2][5].

- Contingency percentages tend to **decrease as project definition improves**: early conceptual estimates may require 20-30%, while detailed design estimates may reduce contingency to 5-10%[1][10].

- Contingency should be **justified and documented**, with clear linkage to identified risks and their probability/impact, and adjusted as project risks evolve[6].

- Typical **contractor contingency fees** range from 5% (low risk) to 20% (high risk/complex projects), aligning with the above risk factor contingencies[5].
