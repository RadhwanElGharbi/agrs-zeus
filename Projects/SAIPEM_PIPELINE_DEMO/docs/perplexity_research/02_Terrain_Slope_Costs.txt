### Supporting Details and Sources:

- **Baseline Costs and Diameter Effects:** Average interstate pipeline diameter is around 30 inches. Costs for smaller diameters (4-10 inches) are less sensitive to diameter changes, with labor and right-of-way costs dominating[1][8].

- **Labor and Material Shares:** Labor can represent up to ~47% of land pipeline construction costs, with materials around 13%, and right-of-way and miscellaneous costs making up the rest[1][7].

- **Slope and Terrain Impact:** Costs increase with slope steepness due to additional earthworks, safety measures, and slower progress. Very steep slopes (>30°) can nearly triple baseline costs[7][8].

- **Special Terrain Types:**
  - *Swamp/Wetland:* Requires specialized equipment like mats and floating platforms, environmental mitigation, and permits, doubling or tripling costs compared to flat terrain[1][7].
  - *Permafrost:* Elevated pipelines or insulated designs to prevent thawing increase costs substantially, often 2.5 to 4 times baseline[7].
  - *Desert:* Logistics and environmental challenges increase costs moderately, about 1.2 to 1.5 times baseline[1].
  - *Urban:* Labor and engineering/administrative costs increase significantly due to underground utilities, traffic management, and regulatory compliance, often 2.5 to 4 times baseline[8].

- **Cost Ranges:** Actual costs per kilometer vary widely by project scale, location, year, and pipeline diameter but generally fall within the ranges above based on historical data and regression models[7][8].
